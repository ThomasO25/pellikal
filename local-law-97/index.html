Local Law 97 & Window Film: A Practical Efficiency Measure

Window film won't make a building compliant on its own — but by cutting solar heat gain and easing cooling load, it's one of the faster, lower-disruption improvements you can add to a broader efficiency plan.

The basics

What is Local Law 97?

Local Law 97 is a New York City law that sets greenhouse-gas emissions limits for many larger buildings, with the caps tightening over time. For a lot of owners, that means finding practical ways to reduce energy use — especially the energy spent heating and cooling the building.

Meeting those targets usually isn't one big project — it's a stack of improvements: lighting, controls, mechanical upgrades, the building envelope and more. Window film fits into the envelope side of that stack. It's not a silver bullet, but because it's quick to install and works on the glass you already have, it can be an efficient contributor to an overall plan. To understand exactly how the law applies to your property, work with a qualified energy consultant or engineer.

Where film contributes

How window film supports energy goals

Less solar heat gain

Solar control film rejects a large share of the sun's heat before it enters.

Lower HVAC load

Less heat through the glass means cooling equipment works less at peak conditions.

Reduced cooling energy

Easing the cooling load can lower cooling energy over the hot months.

Better comfort

Steadier perimeter temperatures and less glare improve tenant comfort.
